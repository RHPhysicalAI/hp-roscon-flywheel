Does more curated data make the same policy better?

The SO-ARM ACT teacher was fine-tuned on 20, 40, 80 and 160 of its own curated successes (one randomly placed cube, ±3 cm), then every checkpoint was scored on the same 50 seeded scenes. Yes — but only past a threshold. Too little data makes it worse.

How many cubes landed on the tray

The task is three cubes. The teacher's failures are almost all 1-of-3 — it places the first cube and fumbles the randomized one. Fine-tuning on 160 turns most of those into 3-of-3.

What the curve says

  • Small-data fine-tuning hurts. 20 and 40 break 14–15 scenes the teacher already solved while fixing 6–7. The policy overfits toward a small subset and forgets.
  • 80 breaks even (8 fixed, 7 broken). 160 is a clean gain: +12 points, 8 fixed against 2 broken, and motion on jointly-solved scenes got slightly smoother (0.0057 → 0.0053 mean joint delta).
  • This is why the pipeline has an eval gate. A gate on "improves on the incumbent" refuses 20, 40 and 80 and promotes 160 — it protects the fleet from a bad retrain as much as it ships a good one.
  • Small rungs are subset-dependent. An earlier 40-episode fine-tune on a different 40 scored 80%; this one scored 56%. Read small-N results as noise, not trend. At 160 the outcome is robust.
  • Statistics, plainly. On the 50 ladder seeds, +12 points was about two standard errors (sign test p ≈ 0.11). So the teacher and the 160 checkpoint were re-scored on 50 new seeds: 72% and 86% — the first block replicated almost exactly. Merged to N=100: 73% → 86%, 20 fixed against 7 broken, p = 0.019. The gain is real and stable across independent scene sets. (The 20/40/80 rungs remain N=50; the ladder line above is drawn at N=50 for every rung so the points stay comparable.)
